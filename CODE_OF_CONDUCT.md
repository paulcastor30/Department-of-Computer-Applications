# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want BSCA and MSCA students and graduates, including first-time contributors, to be able to learn and contribute with dignity. Everyone participating in project spaces must treat others respectfully, regardless of experience, academic level, background, disability, gender, identity, religion, or other personal characteristics.

## Expected behavior

- Be welcoming, patient, and constructive. Basic questions are welcome.
- Critique the work with specific examples; respect the person doing it.
- Accept feedback, acknowledge mistakes, and credit others' contributions.
- Respect privacy, accessibility needs, and different communication styles.
- Keep discussions relevant and avoid using academic seniority to intimidate others.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, discrimination, threats, sexual attention or content directed at participants, personal attacks, deliberate humiliation, doxxing, and publishing private information without permission are unacceptable. Repeated disruption, retaliation against a reporter, and pressuring someone to share private student records are also unacceptable.

## Scope

This policy covers issues, pull requests, reviews, and other project-managed spaces, plus conduct while explicitly representing this project. It does not replace university policies or disciplinary processes.

## Reporting and enforcement

**Private reporting contact: pending maintainer confirmation.** A monitored email address and responsible report handler must be published here before the community launch. An arbitrary contributor email is not a reporting channel. Do not put sensitive reports in public issues or pull requests. This document does not currently promise an operational private reporting channel.

The repository owner, [@paulcastor30](https://github.com/paulcastor30), is responsible for moderation of repository spaces. The launch checklist requires an alternate contact for concerns involving the owner. Reports should describe what happened, where, when, and any relevant evidence; share only information necessary to assess the concern.

Moderators should acknowledge reports, assess context fairly, limit disclosure to people who need it, and protect reporters against retaliation. Depending on severity and repetition, actions may include requesting a correction, removing content, issuing a warning, restricting participation temporarily, or banning a participant. Serious cases may warrant immediate restriction. Moderators with a conflict of interest should step aside. Do not promise absolute confidentiality or an outcome before reviewing a report.

This is a project-specific policy. Review its reporting arrangements in [the launch checklist](docs/open-source-launch.md) before inviting contributors.
